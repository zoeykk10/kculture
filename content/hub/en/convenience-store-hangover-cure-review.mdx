>Written by Zoey and Donghyun Kim

Near the register at most Korean convenience stores, a row of small bottles sits alongside the usual gum and candy. These are hangover recovery drinks — a product category that barely exists in most countries but has an entire developed market in Korea. Dozens of brands compete for shelf space, and the market is large enough that convenience stores dedicate a permanent section to them year-round.

For first-time visitors, the existence of a dedicated hangover drink shelf is often one of the more surprising things about Korean convenience stores. This guide covers what these products are, what the main ingredients do, how the major brands compare, and when to actually take them.

## — What Korean Hangover Drinks Are

Korean hangover recovery products are classified either as health functional foods or as general foods — they are not pharmaceuticals. This matters because it affects what the packaging can claim. Most cannot say they "cure" hangovers or make definitive clinical promises, so the labelling tends to be worded around liver function support, antioxidant activity, or fatigue reduction.

The products come in three formats found at convenience stores:

**Small liquid bottles (100–120mL)** — the most common format. Brands like Yeomyeong 808 (여명808), Condition (컨디션), and Hut Gae Su Condition (헛개수 컨디션) fall into this category. Price range: ₩2,500–₩4,500.

**Effervescent tablets** — dissolved in water before drinking. Sangkwaehwan (상쾌환) is the main brand in this format. Sold in small packets of two or three tablets at convenience stores. Price range: ₩3,000–₩4,000.

**Larger canned or bottled drinks (350–500mL)** — positioned closer to regular beverages and priced lower. These contain similar ingredients at lower concentrations and are meant to be consumed more casually alongside food or alcohol.

## — What the Main Ingredients Do

Four ingredient categories appear consistently across Korean hangover recovery products.

**Hovenia dulcis (헛개나무 열매 추출물)**

This is the most distinctly Korean ingredient in the category. Derived from the Japanese raisin tree, it has been used in traditional Korean and Chinese medicine for centuries. Some animal studies and limited clinical research suggest it may support liver enzyme activity and assist with alcohol metabolism. It appears in most Korean hangover drinks in some form and is the defining ingredient of the Hut Gae (헛개) product line.

**Aspartic acid (아스파라긴산)**

An amino acid associated with the breakdown of acetaldehyde — the byproduct of alcohol metabolism that is primarily responsible for headaches, nausea, and fatigue the morning after drinking. The Condition (컨디션) brand is built around this ingredient. Aspartic acid is naturally present in some foods but is supplied here in concentrated form to support faster clearance.

**Curcumin (강황 추출물)**

The active compound in turmeric. Used as a supporting ingredient in some products for its antioxidant properties and possible liver cell protection effects. Not a primary ingredient in most mainstream products but appears in newer formulations.

**B vitamins (B1, B6, B12)**

Alcohol metabolism depletes B vitamins, particularly thiamine (B1). Replacing them helps with energy recovery and reduces some of the fatigue associated with a hangover. Most products include a B vitamin complex as a baseline component.

None of these ingredients are medically guaranteed to eliminate hangover symptoms. The actual effect depends heavily on how much was consumed, whether the person ate before drinking, body weight, and individual metabolism.

## — How the Major Products Compare

**Yeomyeong 808 (여명808)**

One of the oldest hangover drink brands in Korea, first produced in the 1980s. Hovenia dulcis extract is the primary ingredient. The taste is bitter and medicinal — closer to a herbal tonic than a flavoured drink. People either appreciate the traditional character or find it hard to get down. For first-timers, the taste is the main barrier.

**Condition (컨디션, CJ)**

The most widely distributed brand at Korean convenience stores. Aspartic acid is the central ingredient. The taste is noticeably sweeter than Yeomyeong 808 and easier to drink. Multiple product lines exist — Condition Power and Condition Stick among them. If you want something that goes down easily, this is the default recommendation.

**Hut Gae Su Condition (헛개수 컨디션)**

Combines hovenia dulcis extract and aspartic acid in a single product, which is useful if you want both ingredient profiles without buying two separate drinks. The taste sits between Yeomyeong 808 and regular Condition — mildly sweet with a faint herbal note.

**Sangkwaehwan (상쾌환)**

The tablet format makes it easier to carry and use — one packet fits in a pocket. Dissolves in a glass of water and produces a mildly flavoured drink. Hovenia dulcis extract and B vitamins are the main components. The effervescent format is popular with people who don't want to commit to a full liquid bottle before knowing if they need it.

## — When to Drink Them

The timing question matters more than most people assume.

**Before drinking (most effective)**

Taking a hangover drink 30 minutes before alcohol consumption gives the active ingredients time to be absorbed before the liver begins processing alcohol. The liver function support components — particularly hovenia dulcis extract and aspartic acid — are available when the body first needs them. This is the timing most consistently recommended by the product manufacturers, though it's the least commonly practised.

**During drinking**

Taking a bottle partway through a drinking session is a middle-ground option. The ingredients enter the system while alcohol metabolism is actively happening, which is more useful than taking them after everything has already been processed.

**Before sleeping**

The most common timing in practice. Alcohol continues to be metabolised during sleep, so the ingredients still have something to act on. This is not the ideal timing but it's better than the morning after. The products also contribute to hydration at a point in the night when dehydration is already underway.

**The morning after**

By this point, most of the alcohol has already been metabolised and the hangover symptoms are the result of acetaldehyde buildup and dehydration rather than ongoing alcohol presence. The ingredients are less useful here, though the B vitamin content and fluids from the drink still provide some benefit.

## — Getting More out of Them

Hangover drinks work as one part of a broader approach. Used alone and at the wrong time, the effect is limited.

**Drink water alongside them.** Alcohol is a diuretic and dehydration accounts for a significant portion of hangover symptoms — headache, dry mouth, fatigue. Drinking 500mL of water alongside a hangover recovery drink, or alternating water with alcohol throughout the night, has a more direct impact on the following morning than the hangover drink alone.

**Don't drink on an empty stomach.** Food slows alcohol absorption. A convenience store dosirak, a portion of fried chicken, or even a bag of crackers eaten before or during drinking reduces how quickly alcohol enters the bloodstream and gives the liver more time to process it. The hangover drink's ingredients work better when alcohol absorption is not instantaneous.

**Accept the limits.** These products are supplements, not medicine. When alcohol intake is genuinely excessive, no drink available at a convenience store will neutralise the following morning. The most effective hangover prevention remains controlling the amount consumed.

**Quick Summary**

- Product formats: small liquid bottles / effervescent tablets / larger canned drinks
- Key ingredients: hovenia dulcis extract / aspartic acid / B vitamins / curcumin
- Best timing: 30 minutes before drinking, or during — not the morning after
- Easiest to drink: Condition (컨디션) — sweetest flavour, widest availability
- Most traditional: Yeomyeong 808 — bitter, herbal, oldest brand
- Tablet option: Sangkwaehwan — portable, dissolves in water
