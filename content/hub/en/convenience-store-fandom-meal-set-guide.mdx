Within K-pop fan communities, building a convenience store combination around a favourite artist has become a distinct creative practice. Fans select products based on official team colours, comeback concept themes, or fandom in-jokes — then photograph and share the result. This guide explains what the practice involves, how fans construct the combinations, and how to build one from scratch at a Korean convenience store.

## — What a Fan Meal Set Actually Is

A fan meal set (최애 정식, literally "favourite idol meal") is a fan-designed convenience store food combination. It originates in fan communities on X (formerly Twitter), fan cafes, and Instagram, where fans share their own themed product selections.

The combinations are creative works made by fans, not representations of what any specific person eats. Three main approaches drive the selection choices:

- **Colour-matching**: Products chosen to match the official team or member colour. A purple-coded set might include blueberry drinks, grape jelly, and purple sweet potato snacks
- **Concept-matching**: Products selected to fit the mood of a current comeback or activity theme — clean and minimal, bold and dark, sporty and bright
- **Fandom reference**: Products connected to a specific in-joke, fan-created meme, or shared community memory

The defining feature is that the fan is the author. The combination reflects the fan's creativity and relationship to the fandom, not any verified information about the artist.

## — Product Types That Appear Most Often

Certain product categories show up repeatedly in fan meal set posts because they photograph well and offer clear colour options.

**Drinks (the anchor item)**

The drink is chosen first because it sets the colour tone for the whole set. Products with distinct colour payoff — fruit ades (peach for pink/orange, blue lemon for blue, green grape for mint/green), flavoured milk, and coloured sparkling drinks — are the most common choices. The bottle or can is usually placed at the back of the photo for scale.

**Desserts and sweet snacks**

Macarons, roll cake slices, yakgwa (traditional Korean confection), soft cookies, and individually wrapped jellies are popular because they are small, colourful, and photogenic. Dessert items are often the visual centrepiece of the set.

**Triangle kimbap or dosirak**

Included as the "meal" anchor of the set. Flavour choice is often secondary to the packaging colour or the practical function of completing the meal composition.

**Small-format snack bags**

Used to fill out the visual layout. Fans frequently choose snack packages with colour-matching packaging rather than for the snack itself.

## — How to Build One from Scratch

A practical sequence for assembling a fan meal set at a Korean convenience store.

**Step 1: Set the colour theme**

Decide on one primary colour before entering the store. A single-colour focus produces more cohesive photos. Two contrasting colours can represent team duality or a specific concept but require more careful selection.

**Step 2: Choose the drink first**

Pick the drink before anything else — it is the hardest item to substitute once the colour direction is set. Everything else can flex around it.

**Step 3: Work within a budget**

A typical fan meal set runs 7,000–12,000 KRW for the full combination: drink (1,500–2,500 KRW) + dessert (1,500–3,000 KRW) + kimbap or dosirak (1,000–5,500 KRW) + small snack (500–1,500 KRW).

**Step 4: Arrange for the photo**

Place items on the convenience store outdoor table or a clean flat surface. Face all product labels forward, vary the height slightly, and keep the colour theme visible from the front. Natural daylight produces better results than store lighting.

## — Sharing the Set Online

Fan meal set posts travel fastest within fandom-specific spaces, but some formats cross into broader food content audiences.

**Hashtag strategy**

Fandom-specific tags reach the existing community immediately. Adding general tags — #편의점꿀조합 (convenience store combos), #최애정식 (fan meal set) — extends reach to food content audiences outside the fandom.

**Caption approach**

Explaining the logic behind the combination — which colour, concept, or fandom reference it represents — performs better than a caption without context. Captions that make factual claims about an artist's actual preferences should be avoided, both for accuracy and as a matter of fan community etiquette.

**Platform fit**

X (formerly Twitter) moves fan content fastest within fandom networks. Instagram suits high-quality food photography. Short-form video on platforms like TikTok works well with a format that introduces each product one at a time.
