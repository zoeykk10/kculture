Triangle kimbap (삼각김밥) is one of the highest-volume products in Korean convenience stores. The packaging has a three-step opening system that confuses most people the first time. The flavour range runs from mild to spicy across five or six categories. This guide covers the opening method, what each flavour category actually tastes like, and how to choose if it is your first time.

## — How to open the packaging

The packaging is designed to keep the seaweed (김) separated from the rice until the moment of eating — which is why it feels overcomplicated. The three numbered tabs solve this:

- **① tab**: Pull the ① tab at the top centre downward — this splits the outer wrapper vertically
- **② tab**: Pull the ② tab to the left — removes the left half of the inner wrapper
- **③ tab**: Pull the ③ tab to the right — removes the right half

Done in order, the seaweed ends up wrapped around the rice from both sides. If the tabs are pulled out of sequence or the wrapper is torn open, the seaweed separates from the rice entirely. The numbers are printed directly on the packaging.

## — What each flavour category tastes like

Triangle kimbap flavours fall into four main categories.

**Mayo-based**
- **Tuna mayo (참치마요)**: Canned tuna mixed with mayonnaise. Savoury, mild, and slightly creamy. Generally considered the safest first choice — neither spicy nor heavily salted
- **Egg mayo (계란마요)**: Soft-boiled egg with mayonnaise. Milder and softer than tuna mayo, closer to an egg salad filling
- **Spam mayo (스팸마요)**: Canned ham with mayonnaise. Saltier and heavier than the others in this category

**Meat-based**
- **Bulgogi (불고기)**: Soy sauce-marinated beef, sweet and savoury. No mayonnaise — the flavour sits closer to a sweet-salty glaze over rice
- **Direct-fire bulgogi (직화불고기)**: Same profile as bulgogi but with a more pronounced smoky flavour
- **Jeyuk (제육)**: Gochujang-marinated pork. Mildly spicy — a step up from bulgogi in heat level

**Kimchi-based**
- **Kimchi tuna (김치참치)**: Tuna mayo with kimchi added. Spicy and tangy simultaneously. A reasonable first step into spicy flavours for people who find plain kimchi too strong
- **Kimchi fried rice (김치볶음밥)**: Kimchi fried rice filling. Medium spice level

**Other**
- **Shrimp (새우)**: Light seafood flavour, clean rather than rich
- **Vegetable (야채)**: Vegetable-only filling. The mildest option available

## — Which to try first

A practical order for first-time buyers:

1. **No strong flavours**: Tuna mayo or egg mayo — neither is spicy or heavily salted
2. **Prefer sweet over savoury**: Bulgogi — the soy-based sweetness is accessible without needing prior exposure to Korean food
3. **Testing spice tolerance**: Kimchi tuna first — the tang from the kimchi moderates the heat, making it a gentler introduction than straight kimchi fried rice
4. **Second or third visit**: Spam mayo or direct-fire bulgogi — the salt-forward and smoke-forward profiles are more distinctly Korean in character

## — How GS25 and CU lineups differ

The same flavour name at different chains is not the same product — each store uses its own recipe and ingredient ratios.

- **GS25**: Runs a premium line (1,500 KRW and above) with higher filling-to-rice ratios. The standard line sits at 900–1,300 KRW
- **CU**: New flavour variants and seasonal limited editions appear regularly. Standard line also 900–1,200 KRW
- **7-Eleven**: Core lineup similar to the other two. Regional limited flavours appear occasionally

Premium lines at all three stores run 1,500–2,000 KRW and are noticeably more filling.

- Categories: mayo-based / meat-based / kimchi-based / other
- Price: standard 900–1,300 KRW, premium 1,500–2,000 KRW
