Soju sold at Korean convenience stores ranges from 16% to 25% ABV depending on the product. Mixed with juice or sparkling water, it drops to a drinkable 4–8% — closer to beer or cider in strength. The ice cups sold at the counter make it easy to assemble on the spot without any equipment. This guide covers dilution ratios, which juice types pair well with soju, and how carbonated mixers change the result.

## — Dilution Ratios and Target ABV

![A soju bottle and juice can placed beside an ice cup on a convenience store table — ingredients lined up to illustrate a 1:3 soju-to-juice mixing ratio](/images/hub/convenience-store-k-cocktail-soju-guide/korean-soju-mixing-ratio-guide-convenience-store-1-en.webp)

The ratio of soju to mixer determines the final alcohol content. Three reference points:

- **1 part soju : 3 parts juice** — Final ABV approximately 4–6%. The most approachable starting point; the fruit flavour leads and soju heat stays in the background
- **1 part soju : 2 parts juice** — Final ABV approximately 6–8%. Soju is more present; still sweet but with more edge
- **1 part soju : 1 part juice** — Final ABV approximately 8–12%. Soju flavour dominates; most noticeable for people unfamiliar with the spirit

For a first attempt, 1:3 is the practical starting point. As ice melts in the cup, the drink continues to dilute slightly — which works in favour of a beginner ratio.

A standard convenience store soju bottle is 360mL. One 360mL bottle mixed at 1:3 fills roughly two large ice cups. Buying two juice cans (200–250mL each) alongside one soju bottle covers the full volume comfortably.

## — Which Fruit Juices Work Best

![Peach, green grape, and grapefruit juice cans arranged next to an ice cup — three fruit juice types compared for soju cocktail combinations](/images/hub/convenience-store-k-cocktail-soju-guide/korean-fruit-soju-cocktail-combinations-2-en.webp)

Three fruit juice types show up most often in soju cocktail combinations, each with a different flavour outcome.

**Peach juice**

The sweetest of the three and the most forgiving on a first attempt. Peach's dense fruit flavour covers soju's alcohol edge effectively at a 1:3 ratio. If the sweetness feels excessive, replacing half the peach juice with sparkling water brings it back into balance without losing the fruit character.

**Green grape juice**

Lower in sugar than peach, with a light tartness that pairs naturally with soju. The combination reads as clean rather than sweet-forward. Works at 1:2 or 1:3 without needing carbonation to balance it. A straightforward choice for people who find peach juice too sweet.

**Grapefruit juice**

The bitterest of the three. Grapefruit's natural bitterness compounds with soju's edge, so a higher dilution ratio is worth considering — 1:3 or more. Adding sparkling water to cut the intensity is common. Worth trying if you prefer less sweetness and more complexity, but not recommended as a starting point.

## — Using Carbonated Mixers

![Soju and cider mixed in an ice cup — carbonation rising through the drink at the moment of pouring](/images/hub/convenience-store-k-cocktail-soju-guide/korean-soju-sparkling-cider-mix-3-en.webp)

Substituting part or all of the juice with carbonated drinks produces a different texture and flavour profile.

**Cider (clear carbonated soft drink)**

Soju mixed with Korean cider at 1:3 is a widely consumed combination. The result is crisp, lightly sweet, and carbonated. Carbonation dissipates quickly, so drinking promptly after mixing gives the best result. Lemon-flavoured cider adds a natural citrus note without needing additional juice.

**Sparkling water**

Sparkling water adds carbonation without sweetness. The most flexible use is as a secondary mixer — combine soju with juice first, then top with sparkling water to extend the volume and reduce sugar. Peach juice with sparkling water at a 1:2:1 ratio (soju:juice:sparkling water) is a common format.

**Ade-style mix**

Fill an ice cup with ice, add soju, pour juice to about two-thirds full, then top with sparkling water. Reference proportions: 50mL soju + 150mL juice + 100mL sparkling water. Adjust based on cup size. This format produces a layered, lightly carbonated drink that resembles a convenience store fruit ade in appearance.

## — How to Assemble It at the Store

![Hands pouring soju first into an ice-filled cup at an outdoor convenience store table, juice can beside it ready to add next](/images/hub/convenience-store-k-cocktail-soju-guide/korean-how-to-make-soju-cocktail-4-en.webp)

Korean convenience stores sell ice cups at the counter — typically 300–500 KRW. No other equipment is needed.

**What to buy**

- One soju bottle (360mL standard)
- One or two juice or soda cans (200–250mL each)
- One ice cup (purchased separately at the counter)
- Straw (often available near the register)

**Assembly order**

1. Fill the ice cup with ice — at least halfway
2. Pour soju in first
3. Add juice or soda slowly to avoid carbonation overflow
4. Stir lightly with a straw

When using carbonated mixers, adding soju before the carbonated drink reduces foam. Reversed order causes rapid carbonation release and the drink flattens faster. The same principle applies to adding sparkling water as a secondary mixer.

---

**Quick Summary**

- Base ratio: 1 part soju : 3 parts juice (approx. 4–6% ABV)
- Juice types: peach (sweet, beginner-friendly) / green grape (tart, balanced) / grapefruit (bitter, dilute further)
- Carbonated options: cider (sweet and crisp) / sparkling water (cuts sweetness, adds carbonation)
- Ade-style: 50mL soju + 150mL juice + 100mL sparkling water over ice
- Pour order: soju first, then carbonated mixer — reduces foam loss
