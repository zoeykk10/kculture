Korean convenience stores are open 24 hours, which makes them the default option for late-night eating. The obvious choices — instant noodles, lunch boxes, triangle rice balls — are visible and accessible, but they are not the only ones. Behind the front shelves, GS25 and CU stock a consistent range of low-sugar, high-protein snacks that work as late-night options without the calorie load of a full meal. This guide covers what to look for, which products to pick, and how to put together combinations under 300kcal.

## — What to look for on the label

![Close-up of a Korean convenience store snack nutrition label showing calories, sugar, and protein — three numbers worth checking before buying](/images/hub/convenience-store-low-calorie-late-night-snacks/korean-convenience-store-snack-nutrition-facts-1-en.webp)

Korean convenience store snack labels include a nutrition facts panel on the back of the packaging. Three numbers are worth checking:

- **Calories per serving**: Under 200kcal is the first-choice range for a standalone snack. Up to 300kcal works if combining two or three items
- **Sugar (당류)**: Under 5g per serving for low-sugar. Products labelled 무설탕 (no sugar) contain 0.5g or less
- **Protein**: 10g or more per serving helps with staying full longer — relevant when eating late and not following up with another meal

Once you have checked these numbers on a product once, repeat purchases require no further reading. The same SKU carries the same nutrition data.

## — The low-calorie picks

![Smoked eggs, chicken breast cubes, and string cheese lined up in a convenience store refrigerated section — protein-first low-calorie snack lineup](/images/hub/convenience-store-low-calorie-late-night-snacks/best-low-calorie-korean-convenience-store-snacks-2-en.webp)

These are available year-round at GS25 and CU. Calorie figures are approximate and vary by brand — verify the label before buying.

**Smoked egg / soft-boiled egg (훈제란 · 반숙란)**
70–80kcal per egg, approximately 6–7g protein, near-zero sugar. The most reliable low-calorie option in the store. Found either in the hot case near the register or refrigerated. Two eggs put you at around 150kcal.

**Chicken breast cube snack (닭가슴살 큐브)**
90–120kcal per pack, 15–20g protein. Available in several flavours — original, smoked, wasabi. Individually sealed packs make portioning straightforward. Located in the refrigerated section.

**String cheese (스트링 치즈)**
60–80kcal per piece, under 1g sugar. Individually wrapped, portable, and fills the need for something to pull apart and eat slowly — which helps with pacing late-night eating.

**Roasted squid (오징어 구이)**
100–150kcal per pack, high protein. Long chewing time slows the eating pace. Note that some seasoned varieties contain added sugar — check the label if sugar content matters.

**Tofu snack pack (두부 안주)**
80–100kcal per pack. Plain tofu with a small soy sauce sachet. Less savoury than most snack options, which makes it more of a standalone snack than a pairing.

**Mixed nuts small pack (견과류)**
150–180kcal per bag. Higher calorie relative to the others, but single-serve packs make the portion easy to control. Choose unsweetened varieties.

**Cucumber pickle / vegetable pack (오이 피클)**
10–30kcal per pack. Near-zero sugar in unsweetened versions. Works as a palate cleanser between other snacks rather than a main item.

## — Three combinations under 300kcal

![Two smoked eggs, a chicken breast cube pack, and cucumber pickle arranged on a convenience store table — a under-300kcal late-night snack setup](/images/hub/convenience-store-low-calorie-late-night-snacks/korean-night-snack-combination-low-calorie-3-en.webp)

**Under 200kcal**
Two smoked eggs (160kcal) + sparkling water. Protein-heavy, minimal preparation, no cooking required.

**Around 250kcal**
Chicken breast cube pack (110kcal) + one string cheese (70kcal) + cucumber pickle (20kcal) = approximately 200kcal. Add a zero-sugar drink to round out the eating experience without adding calories.

**Around 300kcal**
Tofu snack pack (90kcal) + roasted squid pack (130kcal) + small nuts pack (160kcal) = approximately 380kcal. Swap the nuts for cucumber pickle to bring it under 300kcal while keeping the protein content high.

## — Zero-sugar drink pairings

![Sparkling water, zero cola, and zero-sugar beer alternative side by side in a convenience store drink aisle — late-night pairing options with no added sugar](/images/hub/convenience-store-low-calorie-late-night-snacks/korean-convenience-store-zero-sugar-drinks-4-en.webp)

The drink choice matters as much as the snack at this hour.

- **Sparkling water**: Zero calories, zero sugar. Lemon or grapefruit flavoured versions add enough taste to feel like a real drink
- **Zero-sugar beer alternatives**: Several Korean brands now produce low-alcohol or alcohol-free beer with significantly lower calorie counts than standard beer. Useful when the goal is atmosphere rather than alcohol
- **Zero cola / zero cider**: No sugar, but the intense sweetness can trigger further cravings for some people — a matter of personal preference
- **Unsweetened iced tea**: Available in black tea and barley tea varieties. Contains caffeine — relevant if eating after midnight

---

**Quick Summary**

- Label targets: under 200kcal per serving, under 5g sugar, 10g+ protein
- Top picks: smoked egg, chicken breast cubes, string cheese, tofu snack pack, cucumber pickle
- Under 300kcal combination: chicken cubes + string cheese + cucumber pickle + zero-sugar drink
- Drink pairing: sparkling water or zero-sugar beer alternative; watch caffeine in iced tea late at night
