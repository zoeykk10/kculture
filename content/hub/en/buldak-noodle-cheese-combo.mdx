Buldak (불닭) is one of the most recognisable convenience store noodles in Korea. The name translates literally as fire chicken, and the original version sits at around 4,404 Scoville heat units. For anyone who wants to try it but cannot handle serious spice, the standard fix is one slice of processed cheese laid across the hot noodles. The cheese melts from residual heat and cuts through the sharpness of the sauce. This guide covers the full ingredient list, step-by-step cooking, and additional ways to reduce the heat further.

## — What makes buldak so spicy

![Samyang buldak original in red packaging and carbo buldak in cream packaging placed side by side](/images/hub/korean-spicy-ramen-buldak-bokkeum-myeon-intensity-1-en.webp)

The original buldak ramen scores around 4,404 SHU on the Scoville scale. That puts it above Shin Ramyun (roughly 3,400 SHU) but well below a fresh jalapeño. The issue for first-timers is not the raw heat level — it is that buldak is a stir-fried noodle, not a soup. There is no broth to dilute the sauce. The capsaicin stays concentrated directly on the noodles, which makes the heat feel more intense than the number suggests.

Samyang produces several versions: the original, Carbo Buldak (cream-based, noticeably milder), Rosé Buldak, and Nuclear Buldak (significantly hotter). If you are spice-cautious, Carbo Buldak is the easiest entry point. This guide focuses on the original version with the cheese combo.

## — What you need

![Buldak ramen packet, cheese slice, and mayo sachet grouped together in a convenience store basket](/images/hub/korean-convenience-store-buldak-cheese-recipe-ingredients-2-en.webp)

All items are available at GS25, CU, and 7-Eleven.

- **Buldak ramen** (Samyang original, 140g single pack) — main
- **Processed cheese slice** (cheddar or mozzarella type, 1 slice) — essential
- **Mayonnaise** (single-serve sachet or small bottle) — optional
- **Boiled egg** (convenience store pre-cooked, individually wrapped) — optional
- **Sesame oil mini packet** (condiment aisle) — optional

One cheese slice is enough to noticeably reduce the heat. Mayonnaise and boiled egg are for anyone who wants to cut the spice further while also adding more substance to the meal.

## — How to cook it, step by step

![Red buldak sauce being mixed into drained noodles in a ramen cooker — just before adding cheese](/images/hub/how-to-make-cheese-buldak-bokkeum-myeon-step-3-en.webp)

1. Open the buldak packet and place the noodles in the ramen cooker. Use slightly less water than the standard cup ramen fill line.
2. After 3–4 minutes, drain as much water as possible. Leftover water dilutes the sauce and changes the texture.
3. Add the sauce packet (and sesame oil if included) and mix thoroughly until the noodles are evenly coated.
4. Lay one cheese slice flat across the hot noodles. The residual heat will melt the cheese within about a minute.
5. If using mayonnaise, squeeze it on top of or beside the cheese and stir everything together before eating.

Start eating while the cheese is still melting — the combination of melting cheese and spicy sauce is where the heat reduction actually happens, not after the cheese has fully set.

## — Other ways to cut the heat

![Mayonnaise sachet next to a carbo buldak packet on a counter — two heat-reduction options for spice beginners](/images/hub/reduce-buldak-spiciness-tips-mayo-carbo-4-en.webp)

- **Use half the sauce packet**: The most direct adjustment. Half the sauce means roughly half the capsaicin. Discard or refrigerate the rest.
- **Add mayonnaise**: The fat content neutralises capsaicin. One to two tablespoons makes a noticeable difference without changing the overall flavour profile much.
- **Eat with a boiled egg**: Works as a palate reset between bites. Particularly effective with dry noodles where there is no broth to alternate with.
- **Switch to Carbo Buldak**: The cream-based version comes with built-in heat reduction from the sauce itself. It is a different product, but the closest thing to a lower-difficulty mode.

**Quick Summary**

- Buldak original: ~4,404 SHU — higher perceived heat than the number suggests because there is no broth to dilute the sauce
- Core combo: buldak + one processed cheese slice; mayonnaise and boiled egg are optional additions
- Cooking key: drain water fully, mix sauce in, then place cheese on top while the noodles are still hot
- Further reduction: use half the sauce packet, add mayo, or switch to the Carbo Buldak version
