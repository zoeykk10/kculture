Korean convenience stores sell large ice cups — 350 to 500ml containers packed with cube ice — for around 700 to 1,000 KRW. The point is not the cup itself but what you add to it: sparkling water, fruit juice, iced tea, or a combination. The resulting drinks cost 2,700 to 4,000 KRW total and are consistently cold without dilution. This guide covers ten combinations available at GS25 and CU, the ratio that makes carbonated versions work, and which ones suit each season.

## — How the ice cup system works

![Large ice cup filled with cube ice next to a bottle of sparkling water on a convenience store shelf — the two-item starting point for DIY drinks](/images/hub/convenience-store-ice-cup-drinks/korean-convenience-store-ice-cup-drink-diy-1-en.webp)

The ice cup sits in the refrigerated section near the drinks aisle. It is sold as a standalone product, separate from any specific beverage. The setup at most GS25 and CU locations includes:

- Ice cup (700–1,000 KRW): 350ml or 500ml, pre-filled with cube ice
- Sparkling water 500ml (800–1,200 KRW): the carbonation base
- Juice or fruit drink (1,200–1,800 KRW): the flavour component
- Optional: iced tea can, yogurt, or milk for non-carbonated variations

The cube ice keeps drinks colder longer than crushed ice and melts more slowly, which means the carbonation lasts. The combination logic is simple: the ice cup provides temperature and volume, the sparkling water provides texture, and the juice provides flavour.

For a broader introduction to how Korean convenience stores work — including the hot case, the 1+1 deal system, and microwave access — the [Korean Convenience Store Guide](/en/hub/korean-convenience-store-food-guide) covers the full setup.

## — Ten combinations worth making

![Blue lemonade and peach iced tea side by side on a convenience store table — colour contrast between the two most popular ice cup combinations](/images/hub/convenience-store-ice-cup-drinks/korean-convenience-store-drink-top-10-2-en.webp)

All ten use ingredients available at GS25 and CU. Ranked roughly by how often they appear across Korean social media and how well they work across seasons.

**1. Blue lemonade**
Sparkling water + lemon juice concentrate + blue syrup. The most visually distinctive combination — the blue colour is the main reason it circulates online. Replace blue syrup with purple sweet potato syrup for a different look.

**2. Peach iced tea**
Peach iced tea can or bottle, poured directly over ice. No mixing required. The easiest entry point for anyone new to the ice cup format.

**3. Grapefruit honey black tea**
Grapefruit juice + black tea base drink + honey water. The bitterness of the tea balances the acidity of the grapefruit. Works best in spring and autumn when the temperature contrast is lower.

**4. Strawberry ade**
Strawberry milk or strawberry juice + sparkling water. Spring strawberry limited-edition drinks make this combination cheaper and better. The carbonation amplifies the strawberry aroma.

**5. Green grape ade**
Green grape juice + sparkling water. Higher fruit content than most combinations, which means the flavour reads as tart rather than sweet. Increasing the sparkling water ratio makes it lighter.

**6. Watermelon ade**
Watermelon juice + sparkling water. A summer-specific combination that depends on the seasonal watermelon juice stocked from June. The mild sweetness of watermelon pairs cleanly with carbonation.

**7. Lemon americano**
RTD americano can + lemon juice concentrate. Coffee base with added acidity. Use a small amount of lemon — too much masks the coffee entirely.

**8. Orange ade**
Orange juice + sparkling water. The most straightforward combination. Most convenience store orange juice is reconstituted concentrate, so a higher sparkling water ratio reduces the artificial edge.

**9. Peach yogurt**
Peach drink + plain yogurt. No carbonation. Pour the yogurt first and add the peach drink slowly to get a natural layered effect. Smoother and heavier than the carbonated combinations.

**10. Milk tea**
Black tea can + milk. No carbonation. Choose a can with a stronger tea concentrate so the flavour holds when milk is added. Works in any season.

## — The ratio that makes carbonated combinations work

![Hand tilting a cup and pouring sparkling water slowly along the side wall — demonstrating the pour method that preserves carbonation](/images/hub/convenience-store-ice-cup-drinks/korean-drink-mixing-ratio-guide-3-en.webp)

The starting point for any carbonated combination is **sparkling water 7 : juice 3**. At this ratio, the carbonation stays active and the fruit flavour is present without overpowering the texture. Adjust from there:

- Sweeter: increase juice to 4 or 5
- Lighter: increase sparkling water to 8 or 9
- Non-carbonated (yogurt or milk base): start at base drink 6 : added ingredient 4

Pour order matters. Add the juice first, then pour the sparkling water slowly down the side of the cup with the cup tilted slightly. Pouring sparkling water directly into juice from above causes it to overflow and lose carbonation quickly.

## — Which season calls for which combination

![Spring strawberry ade and summer watermelon ade on a convenience store table — distinct colour difference between seasonal combinations](/images/hub/convenience-store-ice-cup-drinks/seasonal-korean-convenience-store-drinks-4-en.webp)

- **Spring**: Strawberry ade, grapefruit honey black tea — spring strawberry releases are widely stocked and the acidity suits the weather
- **Summer**: Blue lemonade, watermelon ade, green grape ade — colour and cooling are both priorities
- **Autumn**: Peach iced tea, milk tea — warmer colour tones and softer flavour profiles fit the shift in temperature
- **Winter**: Lemon americano, peach yogurt — reducing carbonation and moving toward smoother combinations makes sense when drinking indoors

Outside summer, most people drink these at the convenience store table rather than on the street. Using room-temperature juice instead of refrigerated juice makes the combination less cold and easier to drink when the weather is cool.
