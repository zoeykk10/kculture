>Written by Zoey and Donghyun Kim

When watermelon juice appears in Korean convenience store refrigerators, summer has officially started. Every year around late May or early June, GS25, CU, and 7-Eleven stock their drink fridges with watermelon juice, watermelon-flavoured fruit drinks, and watermelon soda — and the freezer section reliably fills with watermelon bars. Combine either of those drinks with an ice cup and whatever ice cream catches your eye, and you have a full summer dessert assembled at the store counter, no tools required.

This guide covers what kind of watermelon drinks are available and how they differ, how to choose the right ice cup, the exact method for building the ade, which ice cream pairings work best, variation combinations, and seasonal tips for making the most of the limited run.

## — Which Watermelon Drink to Pick

![Watermelon juice and watermelon soda lined up in a Korean convenience store refrigerator](/images/hub/convenience-store-watermelon-ade-icecream/korean-convenience-store-watermelon-drinks-1-en.webp)

Not every watermelon drink in the convenience store refrigerator is the same product. There are three distinct categories, and each one behaves differently when you pour it over ice.

**100% pressed watermelon juice**

These products contain juice pressed directly from watermelon, with little or no added flavouring. The sugar content is naturally high and the flavour is close to cutting into a real watermelon. When used as an ade base, the fruit character holds up even after sparkling water is added. These products are usually a little more expensive than the other two types, but the flavour payoff is worth it if you want a clean result.

**Watermelon-flavoured fruit drink**

This type uses a combination of actual watermelon juice and synthetic flavouring to hit a sweeter, more consistent taste profile. The sweetness is typically stronger than pressed juice, and the price is lower. It works well poured straight into an ice cup, but adding too much sparkling water will dilute the flavour faster than it would with a pure juice. If you're making the basic ade without sparkling water, this version is a solid option.

**Watermelon soda**

Already carbonated, so no sparkling water is needed. Pour it over ice and it becomes a sparkling watermelon ade immediately. The flavour leans sweet and light. One thing to factor in: the sugar level is already high, and adding ice cream on top pushes it further. If you find overly sweet drinks tiring, this type is better with a more neutral ice cream like vanilla rather than the watermelon bar.

Watermelon products typically run from June through late August. From mid-August onward, popular items start going out of stock and may not be restocked before the season ends. If there's a specific product you want to try, don't wait too long into the summer.

## — The Ice Cup Makes or Breaks the Ade

![Ice cup filled with crushed ice next to a small watermelon juice bottle on a convenience store counter](/images/hub/convenience-store-watermelon-ade-icecream/korean-convenience-store-ice-cup-ade-2-en.webp)

The ice cup is not a minor detail. The type of ice inside determines how long the drink stays cold, how fast it dilutes, and what the texture feels like halfway through.

Each major convenience store chain uses a slightly different ice format:

**GS25 ice cups** tend to use finely crushed ice packed tightly into the cup. The smaller pieces mix with liquid quickly and chill the drink almost immediately. Because the ice-to-air ratio is high, you'll need slightly more liquid than you might expect to fill the cup properly.

**CU ice cups** are more commonly filled with cube-style ice. Cubes melt more slowly, which means the drink stays closer to its original flavour longer without getting watered down. If you prefer a stronger, more concentrated juice flavour from start to finish, the cube ice cup is the better choice.

**7-Eleven ice cups** often use a mix of cube and crushed ice, which sits somewhere between the other two in terms of melt speed and chill intensity. The balance makes it a reliable option when you're not sure which style you prefer.

Ice cup sizes typically range from 300mL to 500mL. For watermelon ade, fill the cup with liquid to about 70–80% of the ice volume. Going past that point risks overflow when the ice shifts. Going well under it leaves the drink too diluted too quickly once the ice melts.

One rule to always follow: ice cups are designed for cold drinks only. Do not pour hot coffee or hot tea into them. The cup material can warp under heat. Keep the ice cup to cold beverages exclusively.

For a broader look at how to use ice cups with different drink types, the [Korean Convenience Store Guide](/en/hub/korean-convenience-store-food-guide) covers the full convenience store layout and how different drink categories are organised inside the store.

## — How to Build the Watermelon Ade

![Pouring watermelon juice into an ice cup beside a sparkling water bottle and lemon juice packet](/images/hub/convenience-store-watermelon-ade-icecream/watermelon-ade-recipe-convenience-store-3-en.webp)

The method is simple, but the order and proportion matter if you want a good result.

**Basic recipe**

1. Take an ice cup from the refrigerated section
2. Pour watermelon juice into the cup — fill to 70–80% of the ice volume
3. Insert a straw and stir the ice once
4. Wait 10–20 seconds for the temperature to drop fully before drinking

**Sparkling version**

1. Pour watermelon juice first, filling to about 40–50% of the ice volume
2. Add a small sparkling water bottle (250mL) slowly — pouring too fast causes overflow
3. Stir once, gently — this preserves the carbonation while mixing the two liquids

**Lemon version**

Convenience stores carry small single-portion lemon juice packets, usually in the condiment or baking section rather than the drink aisle — check both spots. Adding two or three drops to the basic ade cuts through the sweetness and sharpens the watermelon flavour noticeably. In the sparkling version, lemon juice brings it close to a café-style ade in terms of balance.

## — Ice Cream Pairings and How to Eat Them

![Watermelon bar and melon bar placed on top of watermelon ade in a convenience store cup, photographed near the freezer section](/images/hub/convenience-store-watermelon-ade-icecream/korean-convenience-store-food-hack-watermelon-ice-bar-4-en.webp)

The ice cream you choose changes the character of the combination. These four options each work differently with watermelon ade.

**Watermelon bar (수박바)**

The watermelon bar has been a Korean summer convenience store staple for decades. The outer shell is made with watermelon juice, and the design replicates the green rind, red flesh, and black seeds of a real watermelon. Placing a watermelon bar on top of a watermelon ade keeps the flavour theme consistent from the first sip to the last. As the bar melts into the drink, the ade gradually takes on a deeper watermelon flavour. The hard ice cream texture means you can briefly dip it into the drink, which creates an interesting contrast between the cold crunch and the iced liquid.

**Melon bar (메로나)**

Melon and watermelon is a pairing that sounds unusual but works well. The melon's sweetness softens the tartness in the watermelon juice, and the fragrance adds a layer that watermelon juice alone doesn't have. Meronona melts faster than the watermelon bar, so the drink changes noticeably as it goes. Alternating between eating a spoonful of the bar and drinking the ade gives two distinct flavour hits in sequence rather than a blended one.

**Vanilla ice cream cup**

Small single-serve vanilla ice cream cups are available at most stores. Spoon one or two portions into the ade and the texture thickens slightly. The vanilla base doesn't compete with the watermelon flavour but rounds off the sharpness. The drink gets progressively sweeter and creamier toward the end of the cup, which makes for a different experience than a straight fruit drink.

**Strawberry bar**

Depending on the season, strawberry-flavoured ice bars appear alongside the watermelon selection. Strawberry has a tartness that contrasts with watermelon's sweetness rather than blending smoothly with it. The result is less cohesive than the watermelon or melon pairing, but if you find the watermelon-on-watermelon combination too one-dimensional, the contrast is worth trying.

## — Summer Combination Variations

![Watermelon ade with a spoonful of yogurt and a pre-cut fruit cup on top, ingredients spread across a table](/images/hub/convenience-store-watermelon-ade-icecream/watermelon-dessert-drink-variation-5-en.webp)

Starting from the base watermelon ade, a few additions create noticeably different results. All of the ingredients below are available in the same convenience store.

**Tropical ade**

Mix watermelon juice and a coconut drink in equal proportions over the ice cup. The coconut's faint nuttiness pairs well with the light, watery sweetness of watermelon, and the combination reads as tropical without being heavy. No sparkling water needed — the flavour stands on its own.

**Yogurt lassi style**

Spoon one portion of plain or lemon-flavoured yogurt into the watermelon ade. The yogurt's acidity balances the sweetness of the juice and the drink becomes closer to a lassi in texture and character. Yogurt is usually in the refrigerated dairy section alongside milk and cheese. The protein content also makes the combination more filling than the plain drink.

**Watermelon milk**

Mix watermelon juice and plain whole milk at a 2:1 ratio over ice. The result is lighter than banana milk and has a similar concept — a fruit-flavoured cold milk drink for summer. The ratio matters here: too much juice, or a juice with high acidity, can cause the milk to look curdled. Stir consistently while drinking to keep the mix even.

**Fruit cup topping**

Convenience stores sell small pre-cut fruit cups in watermelon, melon, and mango portions, typically near the refrigerated section or close to the register. Setting one on top of the ade adds actual fruit pieces to eat between sips. It's a simple addition that turns the drink into something closer to a fruit dessert. Stock on watermelon fruit cups runs out quickly during peak season — afternoon visits may find them already sold out.

## — Seasonal Tips for Getting the Most Out of It

![Outdoor seating near a Han River park convenience store in summer, watermelon ade on the table](/images/hub/convenience-store-watermelon-ade-icecream/han-river-park-convenience-store-summer-6-en.webp)

**When the products are available**

Watermelon drinks and watermelon ice cream bars generally appear in late May or early June and run through late August or early September. The window is roughly two to three months. Toward the end of the season, inventory at popular locations drops fast and popular items may not be restocked.

**How to check stock**

The CU Membership, GS&POINT, and 7-Eleven apps allow stock checks at individual store locations in some cases. If there's a specific product you want, checking the app before walking to the store can save time. If the app doesn't show that item, calling ahead works too.

**Price reference**

- Ice cup: ₩500–₩1,000
- Watermelon juice, small bottle (200–300mL): ₩1,500–₩2,500
- Watermelon bar: ₩1,200–₩1,800
- Sparkling water, small bottle (250mL): ₩700–₩1,200

A watermelon ade with a watermelon bar comes to roughly ₩3,000–₩4,500 total.

**Han River convenience stores**

Convenience stores near Yeouido, Banpo, and ttukseom Han River parks stock watermelon drinks and ice cream in larger quantities during summer. Many have outdoor seating directly next to the store, which makes assembling and eating the combination outdoors straightforward. If summer in Seoul is on the itinerary, the Han River park stores are where this combination makes the most sense to try.

---

**Quick Summary**

- Drink types: pressed juice (best flavour) / flavoured drink (sweeter, cheaper) / watermelon soda (no sparkling water needed)
- Ice cup fill level: 70–80% of ice volume to avoid overflow
- Sparkling version: juice first (40–50%), then sparkling water slowly, stir once
- Ice cream order: watermelon bar > melon bar > vanilla cup > strawberry bar
- Variation options: coconut mix / yogurt spoon / watermelon milk / fruit cup topping
- Season: roughly June–August; stock drops fast from mid-August onward
