Every major Korean convenience store chain sells its own private-label ramen alongside the manufacturer brands. GS25 has YOUR:S (유어스), CU has HEYROO (헤이루), and 7-Eleven has 세븐셀렉트 (Seven Select). These are store-exclusive products — they cannot be purchased outside the respective chains. Prices run from around 700 to 1,300 KRW, consistently lower than manufacturer-brand ramen at the same stores. This guide covers how the three differ and which suits which preference.

## — What private-label ramen is

PB (Private Brand) means the convenience store commissions a manufacturer to produce ramen to its own recipe, sold exclusively under the store's brand name. The store controls the price point, flavour profile, and packaging.

Key facts for each chain's PB ramen line:

- **GS25 YOUR:S**: Cup ramen and bag ramen both available. Price range 700–1,200 KRW
- **CU HEYROO**: Widest flavour variety among the three — kimchi, seafood, and doenjang (soybean paste) variants alongside the standard. Price range 700–1,200 KRW
- **7-Eleven 세븐셀렉트**: Focused primarily on cup ramen. Price range 800–1,300 KRW

All three are chain-exclusive. If the product is not stocked at the specific chain, it is not available at the others.

## — What a blind comparison actually tests

Removing the packaging and comparing side by side, four things differentiate the products:

**Broth intensity**
Determined by the powder sachet composition. A more concentrated broth has higher seasoning density; a cleaner broth is typically designed around a lighter flavour profile. Neither is inherently better — this comes down to how the ramen is being eaten and what it is paired with.

**Noodle texture**
Both fried noodles (유탕면) and air-dried noodles (건면) appear across PB lines. Fried noodles have more chew and a richer mouthfeel; air-dried noodles are lighter and absorb broth differently. The difference is noticeable when eating plain, less so when adding other ingredients.

**Toppings (건더기)**
The dried topping sachet determines whether the ramen works as a standalone meal. More substantial toppings — larger vegetable pieces, identifiable protein — make the difference between a snack and a meal.

**Price-to-portion ratio**
At similar price points, the actual noodle weight and sachet contents vary. For bag ramen, 120g or above is the standard threshold. Below that, the portion reads as a snack rather than a meal.

## — How each brand differs

**GS25 YOUR:S**
The broth tends toward the lighter end — not bland, but less aggressive than the standard manufacturer brands. The noodle texture is standard for fried ramen. Works well as part of a larger convenience store meal rather than a standalone.

**CU HEYROO**
The broadest flavour selection of the three, which makes it the most practical for repeat visits — less likely to eat the same thing twice in a row. Broth intensity sits in the middle range. The kimchi variant is consistently the most-discussed flavour in the line.

**7-Eleven 세븐셀렉트**
Cup ramen is the primary format. Topping contents are more substantial relative to the price point, making it the most suitable for eating as a single meal inside the store. Slightly higher price ceiling than the other two.

## — Which to choose based on preference

- **Lighter broth, pairing with other items**: GS25 YOUR:S — works alongside other convenience store food without overpowering the overall meal
- **Variety across multiple visits**: CU HEYROO — the flavour range makes repeat purchases more interesting
- **Standalone meal, cup ramen format**: 7-Eleven 세븐셀렉트 — topping volume justifies eating it alone
- **Lowest possible price**: All three run 1+1 promotions regularly. During a promotion, effective cost drops to 700–800 KRW per unit. Check the store app or the entrance poster before buying
